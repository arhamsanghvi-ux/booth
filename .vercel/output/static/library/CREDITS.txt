Booth’s three-stem library.

This is not a dump of the OpenAudio list. OpenAudio
(https://github.com/webprofusion/OpenAudio) is a catalog of open audio
software — apps, plugins, and libraries — not redistributable recordings.
Sibling catalogs (SFZ Instruments, the Open Audio Stack, Awesome JUCE,
smpldsnds) point at the same kind of thing. The sounds below are the
recordings those catalogs actually lead to, trimmed into this desk.

Drums
  Public-domain one-shots from the Sonic Pi sample library (CC0),
  originally Freesound recordings trimmed for Sonic Pi.
  https://github.com/sonic-pi-net/sonic-pi/tree/dev/etc/samples
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
  Kits: 808, club, acoustic, soft, room, vintage, boom, haus, tek, chip,
  fat, heavy, hand (tabla), glitch, pure, zum, gas, sone.

Bass and chords
  Multisampled notes from FluidR3_GM, Frank Wen’s General MIDI soundfont,
  via the gleitz midi-js-soundfonts collection. Free to use, including in a
  mix. The soundfont itself is not for sale as a product.
  https://github.com/gleitz/midi-js-soundfonts
  Basses: finger, picked, upright, synth, sub, fretless, slap, slap II, bowed.
  Chords: piano, rhodes, electric piano, steel guitar, jazz guitar, nylon,
  clean guitar, driven guitar, strings, synth strings, pizzicato, tremolo,
  warm pad, sweep pad, polysynth, organ, church organ, rock organ, choir,
  cello, harp, brass, horn, accordion, harmonica, marimba, vibes, clav,
  honky-tonk piano, tenor sax, flute, pan flute, bells, celesta, music box.

AudioKit
  https://github.com/AudioKit is an MIT synthesis toolkit (Swift, Soundpipe,
  STK). It does not publish a redistributable sample bank, so these stems are
  not AudioKit waveforms. The desk still follows a three-bus layout — drums,
  bass, chords — the way a sampler session is built.

The lyric’s words choose the trio. A scale button, or a written sheet’s
genre, still locks the family when you set one.
